Noise-normalized CTF — what each reconstruction recovers from all pixels

NN-CTF η(Qp) = |Σ w T| / √(N Σ|w|²), summed over the N pixels in the bright-field disk with pixel weights w. The noise model applies to tcBF and acBF: with a flat NPS (as in Ma et al.) every weighted pixel adds noise at each Qp; a vBF mask keeps each pixel's image only inside its sideband disks (single + double sideband) or only where one sideband is present, so only those pixels add noise at that Qp. With the single-sideband mask acBF is identical to SSB. BF, ABF and iCoM/DPC integrate detector signals and SSB is always restricted to the double-overlap lunes, so the switch does not change them. A shot-noise-limited image at fixed dose has SNR ∝ η. The ideal Zernike phase plate in TEM has η = 2, so DQE = η²/4 is the dose efficiency relative to that ideal.

Theory and conventions

The probe is A(K) = Ap(K) exp[−iχ(K)],   Ap = 1 for |K| ≤ Kmax = α/λ. Place it at Rp on a weak phase object, t(r) ≈ 1 + iσφ(r), and record the intensity I(Kf, Rp) on the detector. Fourier transforming over probe positions and keeping first order in σφ gives

G(Kf, Qp) = ∫ I(Kf, Rp) e−2πi Qp·Rp dRp = |A(Kf)|² δ(Qp) + σ φ̃(Qp) T(Kf, Qp) T(Kf, Qp) = i [ A*(Kf) A(Kf − Qp) − A(Kf) A*(Kf + Qp) ]

This is the quantity plotted. Because φ is real, T(Kf, −Qp) = T*(Kf, Qp). |T| ≤ 2 everywhere.

  • Detector space (fixed Qp, image over Kf): the first term lives in the overlap of the BF disk with the disk centered at +Qp, the second in its overlap with the disk at −Qp. These are the double-overlap regions. For |Qp| < Kmax the two shifted disks also overlap each other inside the BF disk (triple overlap). With no aberrations, the two lunes are equal and opposite, which is the in-focus single-sideband (SSB) picture.
  • vBF space (fixed Kf, image over Qp): the first term is nonzero for |Qp − Kf| ≤ Kmax and the second for |Qp + Kf| ≤ Kmax. These are the two sidebands of that pixel's image: single-sideband where one disk is present, double-sideband where both are. For Kf = 0 and centrosymmetric χ, T = 2 sin χ(Qp), the conventional BF CTF.
  • To first order both terms carry the phase ∇χ(Kf)·Qp, the same for each term. That is the real-space shift of each pixel's image that tcBF removes. The quadratic remainder is the CTF that tcBF leaves behind and that aberration-corrected BF (acBF) also corrects.

Aberration function (θ = λ|K|, φ its azimuth):

χ = (2π/λ) [ ½C1θ² + ½A1θ²cos 2(φ−φA1) + ⅓B2θ³cos(φ−φB2) + ⅓A2θ³cos 3(φ−φA2) + ¼C3θ⁴ + ¼A3θ⁴cos 4(φ−φA3) + ⅙C5θ⁶ ]

Combined transfer. For pixel weights w(Kf, Qp), with all bright-field pixels receiving the same Poisson dose, η = |Σ w T| / √(N Σ|w|²). The reconstructions differ only in their weights. Two noise models are offered:

  • Flat NPS (default, as in Ma et al.): each pixel's vBF image carries white Poisson noise at every Qp, so Σ|w|² runs over every pixel with a nonzero weight.
  • vBF mask, single + double sideband: each pixel's image is first Fourier-masked to its two sideband disks, so at a given Qp only the Ns pixels in the double/triple-overlap support contribute noise, and Σ|w|² runs over that support only.
  • vBF mask, single sideband only: each pixel's image keeps only the regions where one sideband disk is present. In detector space these are exactly the double-overlap lunes, so acBF with this mask is SSB, and tcBF becomes SSB-masked tcBF whose peaks touch the SSB curve. The single + double mask above is the same as keeping the double- and triple-overlap regions in detector space: the two spaces are slices of one 4D support, with single sideband ↔ double overlap and double sideband ↔ triple overlap.
  • The switch applies to tcBF and acBF, which are built from individual pixel images. BF, ABF and iCoM/DPC integrate detector signals (a detector cannot mask by Qp), so they always use the unfiltered form. SSB is always restricted to the double-overlap lunes.

Weights:

  • BF: w = 1 for the vBF pixels inside a detector of radius β, |Kf| ≤ β, giving |Σ T|/√(N Nβ). As β → Kmax the BF phase contrast vanishes, as reciprocity requires (a BF detector the size of the probe aperture has no first-order phase contrast). ABF: w = 1 on the annulus from the inner edge to Kmax. iCoM/DPC: w = Kf·Q̂p.
  • tcBF: w = exp[−i∇χ(Kf)·Qp], which removes each pixel's shift but not its residual CTF.
  • SSB: w = exp[−i arg T] on the Nd pixels in the double-overlap lunes only (one sideband present, triple overlap excluded), giving Σd|T|/√(N Nd). This is the aberration-corrected single-sideband method of Rodenburg, McCallum and Nellist, which works on G(Kf, Qp) in detector space.
  • acBF: w = exp[−i arg T], unit modulus, giving Σ|T|/N with a flat NPS, or Σ|T|/√(N Ns) with support filtering. Each pixel's shift and residual CTF are both corrected before summing.
  • Self-luminous limit (dotted): per pixel |T| ≤ |A*(Kf)A(Kf−Qp)| + |A(Kf)A*(Kf+Qp)|, so Σ|T| ≤ 2N𝔸(Qp), where 𝔸 is the normalized overlap area of two aperture disks (the aperture autocorrelation, i.e. the incoherent, ADF-like transfer function). With a flat NPS this gives ηSL = 2𝔸(Qp), the ADF shape, and DQESL = 𝔸², exactly the PCTF bound 𝔸(ω) of Ma et al.; tcBF peaks touch it wherever |sin(½k0Δf|ω|²)| = 1. With support filtering, ηSL = 2𝔸(Qp)/√(2𝔸(Qp) − 𝔸(2Qp)), using Ns/N = 2𝔸(Qp) − 𝔸(2Qp); the gain over the flat-NPS value is the noise removed from pixels that carry no signal at that Qp. Both include the source envelope. The limit is reached when both sidebands add in phase in every pixel, and no aberrations can push acBF above it.

DQE. At fixed dose, SNR² ∝ η². An ideal Zernike phase plate with a perfect detector has η = 2, so DQE(Qp) = η²/4 is the fraction of the dose that a method turns into phase signal at Qp, relative to that ideal. The detector itself is taken as perfect, so this is the DQE of the imaging method. For BF the magnitude |ΣT| is used.

The combined maps are summed on a K grid of the chosen sampling, so large aberrations need finer sampling. The status line warns when the phase changes by more than π/2 between neighbouring pixels. Partial coherence: a Gaussian focal spread of rms Δ shifts C1, and χ is linear in C1, so the incoherent average is exact. Each term of T is multiplied by exp[−½(πλΔ)²(|Kf|² − |Kf ∓ Qp|²)²], which is the double-overlap form of the chromatic envelope. A Gaussian source of rms size σ adds the envelope exp(−2π²σ²|Qp|²).
